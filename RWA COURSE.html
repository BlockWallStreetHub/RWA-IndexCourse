Module 01

What Is a Real World Asset?

Before we get to billion-dollar protocols and stock-paying tokens, we need to understand the core idea — what does it actually mean to put a real asset on a blockchain?

The Basic Idea

Real world meets the blockchain

A Real World Asset (RWA) is exactly what it sounds like: something that exists in the real world — a stock, a bond, a building, a bar of gold, a cow — that gets represented as a digital token on a blockchain.

Think about what owning a stock certificate used to mean. You had a piece of paper — or later, a digital entry in a brokerage database — that said you owned a piece of a company. Tokenization is doing that same thing, but on a blockchain. Instead of a database entry at Fidelity, your ownership lives in a smart contract that anyone can verify, that can move across borders in seconds, and that can plug directly into decentralized finance (DeFi) apps.

The reason this matters: traditional finance has walls. You need a brokerage account to buy Apple stock. You need to be in the right country, have the right documents, meet the right minimums. Tokenization chips away at those walls.

The cow example (this just happened)

In July 2026, a dairy farm in Brazil tokenized 10 cows — giving each one a unique blockchain ID generated from AI sensor collar data — and used them as verified collateral to secure a R$100,000 loan on B3, Brazil's stock exchange. The cows' health and location tracked in real time, no inspector needed. This is RWA tokenization solving a real problem: farmers who couldn't access credit, now can.

The Four Categories

What gets tokenized?

RWAs aren't one thing. There are four main categories, each with different risk, return, and purpose.

Category 01

Government Bonds & T-Bills

The safest category. Protocols like Ondo Finance, BlackRock BUIDL, and Franklin Templeton BENJI take U.S. Treasury bills — the most stable assets on earth — and put them on-chain. Typical yield: 3.5–5% APY. This is what institutions use to park cash and earn yield without leaving the blockchain ecosystem.

Category 02

Private Credit & Loans

Higher risk, higher reward. Protocols like Maple Finance, Centrifuge, and Goldfinch tokenize business loans, mortgages, and lending pools. Yield: 8–16% APY. The catch: if a borrower defaults, you feel it. Not backed by the U.S. government — backed by a company's ability to repay.

Category 03

Tokenized Stocks & ETFs

On-chain versions of equity exposure. You get price movement of stocks like Nvidia or Apple, tradeable 24/7 without a brokerage. Platforms like Ondo Global Markets and Robinhood Chain's Stock Tokens lead here. Important: these are price trackers, not real shares — no voting rights, no dividends.

Category 04

Real Estate, Gold & More

Platforms like RealT let you own a fraction of a rental property in Detroit for as little as $50. PAX Gold (PAXG) is one token backed by one troy ounce of real gold in a vault. Even carbon credits, art, and infrastructure are being tokenized. The category is as wide as the physical world.

Why It Matters

Three problems tokenization solves

Access

A minimum buy-in of $50 for real estate (vs. a $200K down payment). Stocks available in 120+ countries without a local brokerage. Credit for farmers with no bank relationships.

Hours

Traditional markets close at 4pm ET, Monday–Friday. Tokenized assets trade 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year. No waiting for Monday morning.

Composability

Once an asset is on-chain, it plugs into DeFi protocols automatically. Use your tokenized Treasury bill as collateral for a loan. Earn yield while it sits in a lending pool. That's not possible with traditional finance.

Module 03

Robinhood Chain: Wall Street Comes On-Chain

On July 1, 2026, Robinhood — the app that already had 23 million funded users — launched its own blockchain. This is the bridge between the RWA projects we just covered and the token we're about to study.

What It Is

A blockchain built for finance, not speculation

Robinhood Chain is an Ethereum Layer 2 — meaning it sits on top of Ethereum's security but runs faster and cheaper. It's built on Arbitrum's Orbit technology, the same stack powering dozens of specialized blockchains.

The difference from other chains: Robinhood didn't launch a blockchain and then try to find users. They had 23 million funded retail investors who already trusted the Robinhood brand, then opened a blockchain for developers to build on. That's the entire pitch — not better technology, better distribution.

The launch event was called "The World Is Flat"

The name says it all — the goal is to remove the geographic and financial walls that keep most of the world locked out of U.S. financial markets. Stock Tokens are available to users in 120+ countries. A trader in Lagos can now get 24/7 exposure to Nvidia without a U.S. brokerage account.

What Launched

Three products, one chain

Product 01

Stock Tokens

Tokenized versions of U.S. stocks and ETFs — NVDA, AAPL, GOOG, MSFT, SPY, QQQ and more. Trade 24/7, in 120+ countries. The catalog has grown from 200 at launch to 2,000+ tokens. Critical note: these are debt instruments, not equity — no shareholder rights per the SEC's January 2026 guidance.

Product 02

USDG Yield (Robinhood Earn)

Users can earn approximately 7% self-custody yield on USDG (Robinhood's stablecoin) via the Morpho lending protocol — with insurance. This brings DeFi-grade yields to mainstream users without requiring them to understand DeFi at all.

Product 03

AI Agent Trading

Automated on-chain agents that can trade on your behalf, execute strategies, and interact with DeFi protocols. Described as "AI-native" infrastructure — one of the first major retail platforms to bake this in from day one.

The Ecosystem

Who built on it from day one

Robinhood Chain launched with a full ecosystem of protocols already live — not a ghost chain waiting for liquidity.

Uniswap

The largest decentralized exchange — deployed as the primary public AMM (automated market maker). Most trading volume runs through Uniswap on Robinhood Chain.

Arcus (dYdX Labs × Robinhood)

A DEX specifically built for stock tokens and crypto, co-built between dYdX Labs and Robinhood Crypto — combining institutional-grade perpetuals infrastructure with Robinhood's stock token catalog.

Morpho

The lending protocol powering Robinhood Earn's 7% USDG yield. Integrated directly into Robinhood's consumer product — users earn DeFi yields without knowing it's DeFi.

Lighter

A ZK-powered decentralized exchange. Notable: stock tokens on Robinhood Chain are now eligible as collateral on Lighter — meaning you can borrow against your tokenized NVDA position.

Module 04

The Index: A Stock-Dividend Token

Everything in the first three modules — RWA tokenization, the rise of on-chain equities, Robinhood Chain — sets the stage for this. The Index ($INDEX) is a token that uses all of it to pay its holders in stocks, every 15 minutes.

01 — The Big Idea

What is The Index?

The Index ($INDEX) is a crypto token built on Robinhood Chain. Its pitch fits in one sentence: hold the token, and it pays you in tokenized stocks like Nvidia and Apple, automatically, every 15 minutes.

Old model: reflection tokens

Tokens like SafeMoon (2021) taxed every trade and paid holders back in more of the same token. The "yield" was just recycled hype — when trading slowed, rewards died.

New twist: stock dividends

The Index keeps the tax model but pays holders in something outside itself: tokenized shares of real companies. Same engine, different fuel coming out the exhaust.

02 — The Machine

How the loop works

Five steps, running on repeat. Every trade of $INDEX feeds the machine.

Someone trades $INDEX

A trader buys or sells the token on a decentralized exchange (DEX) on Robinhood Chain.

A 3% tax comes off the top — in ETH

Every buy and every sell pays a 3% fee. Trade $1,000 and about $30 goes to the protocol instead of you. Round trip (in + out) costs you roughly 6% before the price even moves.

The fee pool fills up

A smart contract collects all that ETH. Think of it as a vending machine with no owner — it just runs its programmed rules.

ETH gets swapped for tokenized stocks

The contract spends the pooled ETH buying stock tokens — NVDAx, AAPLx and others — from trading pools on Robinhood Chain. The protocol claims to be a top-3 stock buyer on the entire chain.

Holders get paid, pro-rata, every 15 minutes

Pro-rata means proportional: own 1% of the supply, receive about 1% of each payout. The stock tokens land directly in your wallet — no staking, no claiming, no button to press.

↻ Then it repeats. More trading volume → bigger fee pool → bigger stock payouts. Less volume → the payouts shrink. Remember that; it's the whole ballgame.
03 — Key Terms

Words you need for this module

Tax token
A token whose contract automatically skims a fee off every trade and routes it somewhere — to holders, a treasury, or liquidity.
Pro-rata
Split in proportion to what you own. Own 1% of supply, receive 1% of each distribution. Bigger bag, bigger slice.
Fee pool / treasury
The smart contract that collects trade taxes before routing them to stock purchases.
Flywheel
A self-reinforcing loop where growth feeds more growth — and shrinkage feeds more shrinkage.
DEX
Decentralized exchange — a trading venue run by smart contracts (like Uniswap) instead of a company.
Perpetuals (perps)
Futures contracts with no expiration date. rwa.wtf lets you trade tokenized stocks with up to 50x leverage using these.
04 — The Second Engine

rwa.wtf — more than just a token

This is the part most people miss. The Index team also launched rwa.wtf — their own trading platform where users trade tokenized stocks and RWAs with up to 50x leverage, settled on-chain, 24/7. Its fees feed the same holders.

Why this matters: distributions no longer depend only on people trading $INDEX. A real product with its own users could keep the dividends flowing even if token trading cools off. The platform also added an Intel dashboard (rwa.wtf/intel) for researching stocks, ETFs, insider trades, and politician trades, plus 20+ spot assets.

05 — The Flywheel

Two spirals, one machine

The same loop that makes this exciting is the loop that can kill it. Study both directions.

▲ The up spiral

More volume → bigger fee pool → bigger stock payouts → holding looks attractive → more buyers → more volume. The protocol constantly buying NVDAx and AAPLx also makes it a steady bid for Robinhood Chain's whole RWA ecosystem.

▼ The down spiral

Volume dries up → payouts shrink → the "yield" disappears → holders sell → price and volume compress together. This is how nearly every tax token in history has ended. The payout asset is new; the math is not.

07 — Read This Twice

Risks & red flags

01

You are not buying real stock

Distributions are tokenized trackers — no voting rights, no company dividends, no brokerage protections. Your exit is whatever the on-chain pool offers, and liquidity can be thin.

02

The yield depends on volume

If trading slows, payouts shrink. The down spiral is real — it's the historical fate of every tax token. rwa.wtf revenue may soften this, or may not be enough.

03

No public audit, anonymous team

As of this writing there's no published security audit and no doxxed team. The contract that holds and routes fees is a trust point until proven otherwise.

04

The 6% toll

3% in + 3% out means short-term trading is structurally punished. This design forces you to be a long-term holder — which is the point, and the trap.

05

Stacked risk layers

You hold: a weeks-old micro-cap token → paying in stock-token wrappers → issued on a weeks-old chain. Each layer carries its own smart-contract, issuer, and regulatory risk.

06

Research trap: the name collision

Searching "$INDEX tokenomics" mostly returns Index Coop — an unrelated 2020 governance token with the same ticker. Don't mix their data into your research.
